Rules that make it work

  • Progressive overload: when the top of the rep range feels solid on every set, add 2.5–5 lb next session. Strength going up = the plan is working, whatever the scale says.
  • Effort: end each set 1–2 good reps shy of failure. If you could have done 5 more, it was too light.
  • Rest: 2 min on the big lifts (hip thrust, presses, leg press), 60–90 sec on the rest.
  • Lifting won't make you bulky. It's what gives the "toned" look — muscle with less fat over it. The weights should get heavy over time; that's the point.
  • Off days: walks, yoga, whatever feels good. Nothing that trashes recovery.

Recovery — read this part twice

Plasma donation ×2/week — how to make it work with training

  • Donation days are rest days or light days. Never lift before donating, and skip hard training after — you're down a lot of fluid and blood protein, so heavy sessions that day are wasted (and can leave you dizzy). Best schedule: donate on your two off days.
  • Eat protein 2–3 hours before donating (a shake + a real meal), and hit the full 140g every day — each donation takes roughly 15–30g of blood protein your body has to rebuild on top of muscle-building.
  • Hydrate hard: an extra ~1 liter of water the day before and the day of. It's mostly plasma volume you're replacing.
  • Calcium matters: the anticoagulant they use (citrate) binds calcium — your yogurt/cottage cheese/milk staples cover this; don't skip them on donation days.
  • Ignore the scale for ~24 hrs after donating — you'll read 1–2 lb light from fluid loss. Log weigh-ins on non-donation mornings.
  • If you feel run-down or lifts stall, the fix is usually more food and more sleep — but if it persists, ask the center to check your protein/hematocrit levels (they test these anyway).
  • Sleep is your #1 weak point. 4-hour nights actively burn muscle and drive hunger hormones the wrong way the whole next day — a bad week of sleep can erase a good week of training. Aim for a consistent 7.5–8 hours, same bedtime every night, even weekends.
  • Priority order: (1) sleep, (2) protein, (3) training. In that order for you.
  • On-your-feet job is an advantage — it's why your calories get to be 2,200 instead of 1,800. Keep moving.
  • Recomp timelines: expect visible changes in ~8–12 weeks and a different body in 6–12 months. The scale staying flat while your lifts climb is exactly right — take monthly photos, they'll show what the scale can't.
  • If the scale drifts: below 141 for a couple weeks → add a snack. Above 149 and staying there → trim the granola/honey portions slightly. Small moves only.

General fitness guidance for a healthy adult — not medical advice. Check with a physician if anything feels off. Weigh-ins, checked items, and logged weights save to this device only; they don't sync anywhere.
